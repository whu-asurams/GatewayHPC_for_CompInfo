# 2024 Faculty Hackathon
## Teaching Computational Informatics Using HPC Resources
## Wanjun Hu, Albany State University

# 1.	Introduction

In Early September, I was recommended by my friend and colleague, Dr. Ojo, to join this Faculty Hackathon 2024. Dr. Ojo and I both teach at Albany State University (GA). He is teaching a Biology class related to Bioinformatics, and I teach a Computer Science class, Computational Informatics. Both courses are based on DNA sequence analysis. However, each of us has a different focus. For instance, my Computational Informatics class is more about algorithms and coding in codon table, sequence alignment and fragment assembly. He is more balanced on coding, algorithm, and knowledge in biology. 

My major concerns are (1) coding environment such as Jupyter Notebook and datasets that can be used by those algorithms. My original syllabus relies on Visual Studio for Coding and some manually crafted and usually short DNA sequences. Those DNA examples do not reflect the real life datasets, which raises a concern about the level of practice students could learn. I am looking for real datasets that are used in commercial DNA tests or academic researches. Through those datasets, students can have a first-hand experience of what computational informatics is. My goal is to train students to do the jobs that are similar to professionals in DNA test and analysis.

# 2.	Events

Before the 2024 Science Gateway conference, I attended a sequence of 5 workshops, each lasting 2 hours. After each workshop, there was a break out session. I attended each of them for one hour. 

Each of us is assigned two mentors. My mentors are Dr. Charlie Dey from University of Texas at Austin and Dr. LaTasha Roberts from Austin Community College. Both of my advisors provided a lot of helps.  

Both advisors provided a lot of helps. During our conversations, I was referred to a lot of websites that provide datasets in DNA sequences. I redesigned the syllabus of my Computational Informatics class to include an HPC component. In the new HPC component, I included the Jupyter Notebook coding environment and sources for DNA sequences. They also helped me to create a poster that was used in the Science Gateway Conference. 

After those workshops, I understood what the HPC means and how to how to get access to them. In particular, I created an account with www.access-ci.org. That access-ci website is a gateway to the so-called HPC (High Performance Computing) resources. I also created an account with www.globus.org, a website that provides storage resources for datasets. 

After I signed up with ACCESS-CI, I applied for a so-called SGX3 “project” with allocations. Then, I rescheduled my allocation to use half of the points for using the Anvil Server provided by Purdue University.

The next thing is to find the answers for obtaining the right datasets for my class.  

# 3.	Science Community Gateway 2024

During 10/8/2024- 10/10/2024, I attended the Science Gateway Conference. It was a great experience. I met a lot of people, asked a lot of questions. That three-day conference has a lot of presentations by institutes and companies that can provide free resources in High Performance Computing, Data Storage, Server Administration. There were also Case Studies.

I was able to attend a lunch meeting with Dr. Amy Apon, the program director of NSF, Directorate For Computer and Information Science and Engineering (CISE). During that meeting, Dr. Ojo and I were encouraged to work a proposal that can establish a High Performance server at Albany State University. 

I was able to have discussions with staffs from Anvil server from Purdue University. They helped me to solve some technical problems when using the Anvil server for Jupyter Notebook coding environment. With Dr. Song, the director of Anvil Server at Purdue university, we talked about the Jupyter Notebook coding environment, and also setting up a similar GPU+CPU environment at Albany State University. 

I also talked to Dr. Boyd Wilson, from Omnibond (omnibond.com). We had a rather detailed discussion about a GPU+CPU server. Further, he provided a detailed quote of such a server in subsequent email communications. 

I also talked to several other experts, such as Dr. Jeanette Sperthac, the ketnote speaker on Tuesday, Sean Cleveland, Claire Stirm of the SGX3 community. 

# 4.	Using Anvil In Fall 2024

I used the anvil sever for my class in this semester. We login www.access-ci.org. From there, we get access to the https://www.rcac.purdue.edu/compute/anvil. After login, we lunch the Jupyter Notebook server and code there. So far, we had a good experience with everything. We receive prompt technical supports from both ACCESS-CI and Anvil. 

We will soon run algorithms on DNA datasets that are downloaded from other resources.
